DATA & SOURCES

Where the data comes from

Queue history and timeline

The historical research panels use project-level queue data compiled by Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL) from transmission providers, including MISO and NYISO. The two regional project scenario models use quarterly observations through December 2025 and include project age, capacity, and technology. Historical project attributes may reflect later revisions rather than information available at the time. The MISO outcome is an interconnection agreement; NYISO combines agreements and operational projects. The curves accumulate quarterly agreement or operation, withdrawal, and unresolved probabilities. Historical revisions and incomplete records limit the results; the five-year horizon has less recent validation.

The separate MISO project lookup shows a historical one-year operational and withdrawal estimate from the project’s latest available research snapshot, not a current forecast.

Points of interconnection

The MISO POI list uses the public MISO POI layer and a prepared coordinate file. Some coordinates are associated with Homeland Infrastructure Foundation-Level Data (HIFLD) substation name matches; names and locations have not been independently verified. Rows without valid supplied coordinates were excluded. The nearest five are ranked by straight-line distance before routes are calculated. A mapped POI is not proof of available capacity or electrical compatibility. A verified NYISO POI layer is not supplied; select an endpoint manually for New York.

Map and boundaries

The background map uses OpenStreetMap. State boundaries come from the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2025 generalized boundaries. The MISO planning footprint comes from a 2021 public service-territory polygon. New York uses the state boundary as an approximate NYISO footprint. Neither is a legal service boundary or a parcel map.

Routing and geographic layers

PYORPS searches a limited corridor using a screening surface built from 2024 land cover (NLCD), USGS 3DEP elevation for approximate slope, the National Wetlands Inventory, and USGS PAD-US protected areas. The weights represent relative routing preferences, not dollar costs. The supplied wetlands raster’s class legend is not verified, so nonzero mapped classes are treated as a screening penalty rather than identified by wetland type.

Protected-area designations vary and are not all prohibitions. The data do not cover every parcel, land right, tribal area, floodplain, cultural resource, or permit. Routes use a 3 km search buffer and may miss better alternatives. This is early screening, not a siting study.

Transmission line cost

The line estimate applies state and voltage rates from MISO’s MTEP24 Transmission Cost Estimation Guide to the calculated route length. These are exploratory single-circuit estimates in 2024 dollars, including contingency and allowance for funds used during construction. They exclude separate substations and network upgrades. New York has no default rate here; a user-entered per-mile rate can be supplied.

Interconnection rules and background

FERC’s Order No. 2023 explainer and eLibrary provide context on federal interconnection reforms and filings. MISO and NYISO administer their own processes and publish queue information; NYISO’s process overview describes its changes. FERC filings are background references here, not a direct source for the displayed POI coordinates or the current timeline models.
